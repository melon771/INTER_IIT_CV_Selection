# CIFAR-10N CV Bootcamp: Report (Yash Shende, 24b0360)

**Q1.** Training noise is 40.24% (18,109/45,000). The noise is concentrated in visually similar pairs. Automobile↔truck alone is 13.1% of all errors (truck→automobile 1,247, automobile→truck 1,126). Next come cat↔dog (9.7%), deer↔horse (5.1%), airplane↔ship (4.1%) and deer↔dog (3.7%). Cat and deer are the noisiest true classes (~51% wrong), and horse is the cleanest (28%). The confusion matrix is strongly asymmetric and class-dependent, unlike uniform noise.

**Q2.** Memorization starts around epoch 40. Through epochs 1–33, accuracy on mislabeled training samples stays flat at 11–16% while correct-subset accuracy climbs to ~87%. The model fits clean patterns first and predicts the true class for mislabeled images. From epoch 40, mislabeled accuracy rises steadily (21% → 34% at epoch 49 → 68% at epoch 60) as the LR decays. Noisy validation accuracy peaks at epoch 41 (55.46%; clean val 79.3%) and then falls to 49.34%. I early-stopped at epoch 41 using noisy validation only.

**Q3.** The best signal was 1 − P(noisy label), averaged over pre-memorization checkpoints (epochs 25–41) of the noisy CE run. It gave AUROC 0.956 and precision@k 86.2%. Averaging checkpoints smooths epoch-to-epoch instability. Failure mode: systematic, plausible confusions are under-scored. Of the 2,503 missed noisy samples, most are truck→automobile (596), deer→horse (310) and cat↔dog (556). The network partially learns these human-style label flips as genuine class overlap. Ambiguous clean cat, dog and deer images are falsely flagged.

**Q4.** CSR (relabel when score > 0.65 and ensemble confidence > 0.70, then retrain with CE) was best at 56.04% noisy val vs 49.34% for CE and 55.98% for SCE. It corrected 1,862 labels, and 99.3% of those new labels match the true class. This removes the easiest-to-identify wrong labels, so less noise is memorized late in training. Gains are modest because only ~10% of the 18k noisy labels qualify. The remaining structured noise (mostly confusable pairs) is still memorized late in training.

**Q5.** Yes. On real noise, CSR beat CE by 6.7 points (56.04 vs 49.34). On synthetic noise, the gap vanished (43.26 vs 43.88). With uniform noise, the early ensemble is far less confident (mean max-probability 0.50), so only 294 samples passed the relabel threshold. Those were all correct, but too few to matter. Random flips also carry no learnable structure, so CE memorizes them later but just as completely. Synthetic noise was easier to detect (AUROC 0.987 vs 0.956) yet hurt accuracy more, because it spreads errors across all class pairs.
